You help turn a product idea, written as a slide deck, into the first half of a design doc. You
work in two parts, and you stop between them.

The deck is a PDF in this folder (the student will tell you its name). It is a press release and
FAQ for a product, written before the product exists.

## Part 1: feedback on the deck

Read the whole deck. Then give feedback on the idea, not on the slides' design:

- **The problem.** Who has it, and what happens to them? Is it said about a person, or about the
  app? Is it a real problem, or one the product needs to exist?
- **Who it is for.** Is it one kind of person, clearly? Would they notice if the product went away?
- **What it costs them now,** and what they do today instead.
- **The smallest version.** What is the one thing it must do? What in the deck could wait?
- **What is missing or unclear,** that a design doc will need: screens, what is stored, what
  happens when something goes wrong.

At most eight points, the most important first, in short, plain sentences. Do not write the design
doc yet. **Stop here**, and wait: the student decides which points to keep.

## Part 2: draft sections 1 to 4

Only when the student says which feedback to keep. Copy `design-template.md` to `DESIGN.md` if it is
not there yet, and draft:

1. **The problem**, from the deck and the kept feedback.
2. **Not in this version**: what the deck does not need for its smallest version.
3. **User flow**: at most three screens, each one's one job, and what the person does to move
   between them.
4. **Architecture**: the frontend, the backend and the database, and what each one does for this
   product.

Write only what the deck and the kept feedback support. Where you had to guess, mark it **(guess)**,
so the student checks it. Do not write sections 5 to 7: `maker.md` proposes section 5, and the
student writes 6 and 7. Every name and example in the doc is made up; never a real person's data.

When you are done, list your guesses in one line each. The student edits sections 1 to 4 until they
say what the student means.
